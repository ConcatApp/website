## Where the button is

**Generate Captions** is the **T** button on the timeline tray, right after the magnetic switch.
Right-clicking a clip with sound also offers **Generate captions**, with that clip already
chosen. On a phone it is **Captions** on the bottom strip.

## Caption a clip

1. Select the clip with the voice in it on the timeline. A video with its own sound works, and
   so does a separate audio clip, such as a voiceover.
2. Click **Generate Captions**. With a clip with sound selected, the sheet opens with **Use voice
   from timeline** on and that clip chosen. The list offers every clip on the timeline that has
   sound, named by its file and where it starts.
3. Pick a **Model**. **Base (English)** is the sweet spot; the sheet lists whichever Whisper
   models you have downloaded.
4. Choose a **Placement** (Bottom, Centre or Top) and a **Size** (Small, Medium, Large).
5. Click **Generate captions**.

<Figure
  src={sheet}
  alt="The Captions sheet: a Transcriber section with a Model picker set to Base (English), a Captions section with Placement and Size, and a Generate captions button"

  The Captions sheet, opened with the voice clip selected.

The sheet reads "Loading the model", then "Transcribing" with a percentage. Nothing leaves your
machine at any point; a 15-second clip takes a few seconds on the Base model.

> [!NOTE]
> **New in 0.2.8 (coming in the next release).** The bar moves through each stage ("Reading the
> clip's sound", "Loading the model", "Transcribing") and says "About m:ss left". **Cancel** is
> reported as a cancel, not a failure, and transcription leaves two processor threads free so
> playback keeps up while it runs. On Windows and Linux PCs, 0.2.7 transcribes with code built for
> older processors only, which makes long clips several times slower on a recent CPU; 0.2.8 picks
> the fastest code the processor can run.

When it finishes, one title per phrase spoken lands on the first lane with room, cut to the
words' own timing, and a toast says how many captions were added.

> [!NOTE]
> If the sheet says "Download a transcriber model in Settings › Transcriber first", open
> Settings from the gear at the top right, go to **Transcriber**, and click **Download** on
> **Base (English)** (148 MB). Multilingual variants detect the language on their own; the
> Small models are more accurate and a few times slower. See [the AI models](/guides/offline-models).

## Caption from a script

Turn **Use voice from timeline** off (it starts off when no clip with sound is selected) and the
sheet shows a **Script** box instead of the model picker. Type or paste the words, choose
placement and size, and click **Add captions**: one title per sentence, each held for as long as
it takes to read, one after another from the playhead.

## The floating Captions panel

> [!NOTE]
> **New in 0.2.8 (coming in the next release).**

On a desktop, Captions is no longer a sheet that dims and freezes the editor. It is a card that
floats over the window: the timeline, the playhead and the selection keep working under it, you
drag it by its header, and it stays where you put it for the session. Its close button hides the
card and leaves a running transcription going; open it again to see the run. If a run fails
while the card is closed, a notice says so. <kbd class="kbd">Escape</kbd> closes it.

With the card open, the clip it captions follows the selection: select another clip with sound
and that is the one shown and transcribed. On a phone, or in a window too narrow for the card,
Captions stays a sheet.

## Import and export SRT

> [!NOTE]
> **New in 0.2.8 (coming in the next release).**

The Captions sheet has **Import SRT…** and **Export SRT…**.

- **Import SRT…** reads a SubRip `.srt` file and lays each cue down as a title at the time the
  file gives it, with the sheet's placement and size. The whole import is one undo step.
- **Export SRT…** writes a lane of titles as a `.srt`: the lane of the selected title, or, with
  no title selected, the lane with the most titles, and the notice says which. The file is named
  after the project. On Android it goes to **Download/Concat**; on an iPhone to the app's
  **Exports** folder in Files.

## Fix a word

Captions are ordinary titles. Click one on the timeline and its words are in the **Content**
box of the Inspector's **Text** tab; fix the word there. Drag the clip's edges to retime it.

## Restyle every caption at once

1. Click one caption, then <kbd class="kbd">Shift</kbd>-click the others (or drag a box around
   the lane) to select them all.
2. The Text tab reads "Editing N titles". Change the font, size, colour, shadow or background
   plate: it applies to all of them in one undo step.

To make that look the default for every caption and title you add from now on, click **Use for
new titles** under the Font picker. Generated captions use that kept style.

## Why a word is wrong

Whisper hears what it hears: product names, names of people and anything mumbled come out as the
closest common word. The Small models get more right; a quiet room helps more. Fix the odd word in
the Content box rather than reaching for a bigger model every time.
