Editing short-form video on a computer is faster than on a phone once the clips pile up: a real
timeline, a keyboard, and a big preview. Here is the whole job in Concat, from an empty project to
a file ready for TikTok, Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts, with no watermark on it.

## Start a 9:16 project

Click **New project**, pick the **9:16** shape and **1080p**. Concat names sizes by the short
edge, so that is 1080 by 1920, the full-screen vertical size every short-form app uses. Keep the
frame rate at what your footage was shot at (30 fps for most phones). See
[your first edit](/guides/first-edit) for the rest of the sheet.

Already cut a 16:9 version? Add a second timeline to the same project instead (the plus beside
the timeline tabs) and set it to 9:16 with the Inspector's **Modify** button. Both cuts live in
one project.

## Bring the footage in and cut it tight

Import your clips (<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+I</kbd>, <kbd class="kbd">⌘I</kbd> on a Mac) and
double-click each to drop it at the playhead. Then cut hard: short videos live or die in the
first two seconds.

- Put the playhead on a pause and press <kbd class="kbd">Q</kbd> or <kbd class="kbd">W</kbd> to
  delete the part of the clip left or right of it.
- With the **Magnetic timeline** switch on, what follows slides up into every gap you leave.
- Speed a slow stretch up from the Inspector's **Speed** section.

The [timeline guide](/guides/cut-on-the-timeline) has every cutting tool.

## Fill the frame

A clip shot sideways sits in the middle of a tall frame with black above and below. Select it,
open the Inspector's **Video** tab and set **Canvas** to **Blur**: the clip's own picture fills
the bars, blurred. Or press **Fill** in the Transform section to enlarge the clip until it covers
the frame. [Vertical video and the canvas](/guides/vertical-video) explains both.

## Add captions

Most people watch short videos with the sound off, so captions are not optional. Select the clip
with the voice, click the **T** on the timeline tray, pick a model and press **Generate
captions**. Whisper transcribes on your machine and one title per phrase lands on the timeline,
timed to the words. Restyle them all at once from the Inspector. See
[auto-captions](/guides/auto-captions).

Place them in the middle of the frame or a little above the bottom: the apps draw their own
buttons and descriptions over the lowest part of the screen and the right edge.

## A voiceover, recorded or generated

Talk over the edit while it plays with [Record a voiceover](/guides/record-a-voiceover), or type
the script into [text to speech](/guides/text-to-speech) and let a built-in voice read it, or a
clone of your own from a few seconds of a recording. Check the length first with the
[script length counter](/tools/script-length-counter): at a brisk pace, a 30 second video holds
about 85 words.

Turn on the three switches in the Inspector's **Audio** tab (normalize loudness, reduce noise,
enhance voice) on every voice clip; see [clean up a voice](/guides/clean-up-a-voice).

## Export

Click **Export**. The sheet starts at the timeline's size (1080 × 1920) and frame rate with a
bitrate fit for upload. Leave the codec on **H.264**: every app accepts it and re-encodes it on
upload anyway, so a newer codec buys nothing here. The file has no watermark and no end card.
The [export guide](/guides/export) covers the rest, and
[H.264, HEVC or AV1](/guides/h264-hevc-or-av1) explains the codec choice.

## A checklist before you post

- The first second shows the payoff, or promises it.
- Captions sit clear of the bottom and the right edge, and nothing reads badly.
- The voice is loud and clean, the music under it.
- The file is 1080 × 1920 and H.264.
