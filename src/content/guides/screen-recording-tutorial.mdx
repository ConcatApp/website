A raw screen recording is mostly waiting: loading bars, a cursor looking for a button, a sentence
started twice. Editing is mostly taking that out. Here is a routine in Concat that turns a
recording from OBS, the Xbox Game Bar, macOS's screenshot tool or any other recorder into a
tutorial.

## Make the project match the recording

Start a project with the recording's shape and size, usually 16:9 at 1080p or 1440p, and the
frame rate it was captured at. Not sure what the recorder wrote? Drop the file on the
[video file inspector](/tools/video-info) to see its size, frame rate and codec. Import the
recording and double-click it onto the timeline.

## Cut the dead air

Play through at your own pace and remove everything nobody needs to watch.

- At the start of a pause, press <kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+B</kbd> (<kbd class="kbd">⌘B</kbd> on a
  Mac) to split; at its end, split again; select the piece and press
  <kbd class="kbd">Delete</kbd>. With the **Magnetic timeline** switch on, the rest slides up.
- <kbd class="kbd">Q</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">W</kbd> delete the part of the clip before or after
  the playhead in one stroke, which is faster for trimming the ends of a take.
- A long wait you cannot cut, such as an install, can play faster instead: split it off and raise
  its **Rate** in the Inspector's Speed section.

[Cut on the timeline](/guides/cut-on-the-timeline) has every tool.

## Zoom in on what matters

Small text in a full-screen capture is unreadable on a phone. Animate a zoom instead of making
the viewer squint:

1. Put the playhead just before the moment and click the diamond beside **Scale** and beside
   **Position X** and **Y** in the Inspector. That sets the starting keys.
2. Move the playhead half a second on, raise **Scale** and move the position so the area you
   want fills the frame. New keys are set for you.
3. Near the end of the moment, add keys at the same values, and half a second later return
   Scale and Position to where they began.

Ease each move in and out, or draw its curve, in the curve editor; see [keyframes](/guides/keyframes).

## Titles and steps

A title per step helps people find their place. Open the Library's **Text** page, pick a style,
and type "Step 1: install" or whatever the step is; drag it to a corner. Style one and then style
the rest at once. See [titles and text](/guides/titles-and-text).

## A clean voice

Record the narration after the cut, when you know what is on screen:
[Record a voiceover](/guides/record-a-voiceover) records over the edit while it plays. If you
talked during the capture, turn on **Normalize loudness**, **Reduce noise** and **Enhance voice**
in the Inspector's **Audio** tab to take the fan noise and the keyboard out from under it; see
[clean up a voice](/guides/clean-up-a-voice).

Prefer not to narrate? Write the script, check its length with the
[script length counter](/tools/script-length-counter), and have
[text to speech](/guides/text-to-speech) read it.

## Captions

Captions make a tutorial searchable by eye and usable with the sound off. Select the voice clip
and press the **T** on the timeline tray; [auto-captions](/guides/auto-captions) transcribe it on
your machine.

## Export

Click **Export**, keep the timeline's size and H.264, and the bitrate it starts at. For a
recording full of small text, export at the size it was captured at rather than smaller, so the
text stays sharp after the platform re-encodes it.
