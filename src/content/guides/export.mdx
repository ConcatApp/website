Click the blue **Export** button at the top right of the window, press <kbd class="kbd">⌘E</kbd>
(<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+E</kbd>), or use File › Export. On a phone, Export is in the top bar.

The sheet flows into one, two or three columns depending on the window. Its sections:

## Preview and Summary

The current frame with a strip that scrubs the playhead, and the facts: the format you are about
to write ("1920 × 1080 · 30 fps · H.264"), the duration, and how many clips and titles are in the
cut. When a hardware encoder will be used, the encoding reads "· hardware".

## Output

| Row               | Choices                                                                                                                                                          | Leave it at                                                        |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Resolution**    | A plain label: the timeline's own size, which is the size the file is written at. A 9:16 edit reads 1080 × 1920. Change it with the timeline's **Modify** button | The timeline's own                                                 |
| **Frame rate**    | The timeline's own first, then every other rate                                                                                                                  | The timeline's own                                                 |
| **Codec**         | **H.264** ("Plays everywhere"), **HEVC** ("Half the size"), **AV1** ("Smallest"). An encoder the machine lacks reads "Not on this device"                        | H.264 for anything you will upload or send; HEVC to halve the file |
| **10-bit colour** | On or off                                                                                                                                                        | Off, unless the footage is 10-bit or HDR                           |
| **Color**         | Only on an HDR timeline: HDR (HLG or PQ, "As the timeline") or SDR ("Tone-mapped, plays anywhere")                                                               | SDR unless you know the player does HDR                            |
| **Bitrate**       | A number in Mbps: drag it, step it or type one                                                                                                                   | What it starts at                                                  |

## Bitrate

The **Bitrate** starts at the rate YouTube asks uploads at for the frame and frame rate (5 Mbps
at 720p, 8 at 1080p, 16 at 1440p, 40 at 4K, half again above 30 fps; less for HEVC and AV1, a
quarter more at 10-bit) and follows them until you drag, step or type another. Every encoder,
hardware ones included, keeps to it as an average, so the size on the Export button is close to
the size written. That rate is right for YouTube, TikTok and anything that gets re-encoded on
upload, which is everything. Files are always written in video (limited) range; there are no
quality cards, no CBR and no colour range switch.

## Destination

A **Name**, a **Browse** button to pick the folder (the Movies folder by default), and the full
path under them. The file is always an `.mp4`. If one with that name is already there, the
sheet asks before replacing it, and a name that cannot be a file name is refused.

On a phone there is no Browse. Android puts the finished file in the phone's **Movies** folder,
where the gallery shows it. An iPhone adds it to Photos and keeps a copy in the app's
**Exports** folder, which the Files app shows.

## Run it

The button at the bottom reads **Export · ~size**. While it runs the sheet shows the stage
(Rendering video, Mixing audio, Finalising file), a percentage and the time left, with
**Cancel export**. When it is done: **Show in folder** and **Export again**.

## Speed and hardware

On a Mac the encoders are VideoToolbox. On Windows and Linux, NVENC, Quick Sync and AMF encode
HEVC and AV1 when the machine has the chip, ahead of the software encoders. 4K 60 10-bit is the
ceiling. An export whose GPU device dies carries on, on a fresh device or then the software
adapter, and the frame that came back black is drawn again.

## Just the sound

Right-click a clip and choose **Save Audio As…** to render that clip's sound to a WAV.
