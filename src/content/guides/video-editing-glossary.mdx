Editing has its own vocabulary, and export screens assume you know it. Here is what the common
terms mean, with a pointer to where each one turns up in Concat.

## The picture

**Resolution.** The size of the picture in pixels, width by height: 1920 × 1080 is "1080p", 3840
× 2160 is "4K". Concat names sizes by the short edge, so a vertical 1080p video is 1080 × 1920.

**Aspect ratio.** The shape of the frame, width to height: 16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for TikTok,
Reels and Shorts, 1:1 square. The [aspect ratio calculator](/tools/aspect-ratio-calculator) works
out sizes for any ratio.

**Frame rate (fps).** How many pictures a second the video shows. 24 looks like cinema, 30 is
usual for phones and the web, 60 is smooth and suits games and sport. Keep a project at the rate
the footage was shot at.

**10-bit colour.** Each colour channel stored with 1,024 levels instead of 256, which avoids
banding in skies and gradients. It matters for footage shot in 10-bit or HDR; Concat's Export
sheet has a switch for it.

**HDR.** High dynamic range: brighter highlights and deeper shadows than standard video, on screens
that can show them. A video in HDR looks wrong on a player that does not support it unless it is
tone-mapped back to SDR (standard range).

## The file

**Container.** The file format: MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM. It holds the picture, the sound and the
captions as separate streams.

**Codec.** How a stream is compressed. H.264, HEVC and AV1 for the picture; AAC, Opus and MP3 for
the sound. See [H.264, HEVC or AV1](/guides/h264-hevc-or-av1).

**Bitrate.** How many bits each second of video takes, in megabits per second (Mbps). Higher
means more detail kept and a bigger file. Size is bitrate times length: the
[file size calculator](/tools/video-file-size-calculator) does the sum.

**Export (render).** Turning the edit into a finished video file. Nothing on the timeline is
changed by it.

**Proxy.** A small, easy-to-decode copy of a heavy clip that the editor plays while you work, and
swaps for the original at export. Concat makes proxies so 4K scrubs smoothly.

## The timeline

**Timeline.** The strip where the edit happens, time running left to right. A Concat project can
hold several, each its own cut.

**Track (lane).** A row of the timeline. Clips on higher lanes draw over the ones below, so titles
and overlays go above the footage.

**Clip.** One piece of media placed on the timeline, which can be a part of a longer file.

**Playhead.** The vertical line marking the frame you are looking at.

**Split.** Cutting a clip in two at the playhead (<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+B</kbd>,

<kbd class="kbd">⌘B</kbd> on a Mac).

**Trim.** Shortening a clip by dragging its start or end edge.

**Ripple delete.** Deleting a clip and closing the gap it leaves, so everything after it moves up.
Concat's **Magnetic timeline** switch makes every delete ripple.

**Main track.** The bottom picture lane, which the rest of the edit is built on. In Concat it is
the **main lane**, and the canvas background is drawn behind it.

## Editing technique

**A-roll and B-roll.** A-roll is the main footage, usually someone talking. B-roll is the
supporting shots cut over it to show what they are talking about.

**J-cut and L-cut.** Letting the sound and the picture of a cut change at different moments. In a
J-cut you hear the next shot before you see it; in an L-cut the sound of the previous shot carries
on over the next one. Both make cuts feel smoother. In Concat, detach a clip's audio (right-click,
**Detach audio**) to move the two edges apart.

**Jump cut.** A cut inside one continuous shot, so the subject seems to jump. Used on purpose in
talking-head videos to remove pauses.

**Picture-in-picture.** A smaller video placed over the main one, such as a face over a screen
recording. A clip on a lane above the main one, sized and placed in the Inspector.

## Animation and effects

**Keyframe.** A value pinned to a moment, such as a scale of 100 at one second and 150 at two
seconds. The editor animates between keys. Concat puts a diamond beside every knob that can be
animated; see [keyframes](/guides/keyframes).

**Easing.** How an animation speeds up and slows down between keys. Ease in and out looks natural;
linear looks mechanical.

**Key frame (in a file).** A different thing with the same name: a complete picture stored in
the video, from which the frames after it are built. Trimming without re-encoding can only start
on one, which is why some quick trims are not frame-exact; see [Trim a video](/tools/trim-video).

**Chroma key (green screen).** Making one colour transparent, so a subject filmed in front of a
green backdrop can be placed over anything. Concat also removes backgrounds without a green
screen; see [remove a background](/guides/remove-a-background).

**Mask.** A shape that decides which part of a clip shows. See [mask a clip](/guides/mask).

**Transition.** An effect between two clips, such as a dissolve or a wipe, instead of a straight
cut.

**LUT.** A look-up table, a file that maps colours to other colours to give footage a graded look.
Concat's Library has an **Adjustments** page with an adjustment layer and your imported LUTs.

## Sound

**Normalize (loudness).** Raising or lowering a clip so it plays at a standard loudness. One of
Concat's three voice switches; see [clean up a voice](/guides/clean-up-a-voice).

**Noise reduction.** Taking steady background sound such as hiss, hum and fans out from under a
voice.

**Captions and subtitles.** Text of what is said, timed to the speech. Open (burned-in) captions
are part of the picture; closed captions are a separate file the viewer can turn on, usually SRT
or WebVTT. The [subtitle converter](/tools/subtitle-converter) converts between the two.
