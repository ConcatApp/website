## Where the button is

**Text to Speech** is the speaker button on the timeline tray, after Generate Captions; a second
press puts the panel away. It is also in the **File** menu as **Text to speech…**, and on a phone
it is **Speak** on the bottom strip. A voice clicked on the Library's
[Audio page](/guides/sounds-and-voices#voices) opens it on that voice. There is no keyboard
shortcut.

## Generate a voice

1. Put the playhead where the voice should start. If a title is selected, its words pre-fill
   the script and the voice lands at that title's start instead.
2. Click **Text to Speech**.
3. Type the narration into **Script**. The line under the box estimates the length of the read.
4. Pick a **Model** and a **Voice** (see below).
5. Click **Generate speech**.

  src={sheet}

The bar names its stage ("Reading the voice recording", "Loading the voice model", then
"Generating voice") and says how long is left. When it is done, a toast says "Voice added to the
timeline": the read is a WAV in your project's `audio` folder, it is in the Library under
**Generated › Speech**, and it sits as a clip on the first free lane at the playhead.

On a desktop, Text to speech is a card that floats over the editor rather than a sheet that dims
and freezes it, like the [Captions panel](/guides/auto-captions#the-floating-captions-panel): the
timeline stays usable under it, you drag it by its header, and closing it leaves a running read
going. On a phone it is a sheet.

> [!NOTE]
> If the panel says "Download a voice model in Settings › Speech first", open Settings from the
> gear at the top right, go to **Speech** and click **Download** on a model. Kokoro (compact,
> 132 MB) is the quick one to start with.

## The three models

| Model                | Good for                                                                                                 | Voices                                                                    | Size         |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Kokoro**           | Built-in speakers, fast. English and Chinese. Has Speed and Pauses sliders.                              | 36 named speakers, American, British and Chinese, female and male         | 132 MB       |
| **Pocket TTS**       | Any voice from a few seconds of a recording. English. Quick, Balanced or Fine quality.                   | Bria and Loona, two recordings that ship with it, or any clip in your bin | 98 MB        |
| **Chatterbox Turbo** | The closest to a studio read. Reads at its own pace and acts out `[laugh]`, `[chuckle]`, `[sigh]`. Slow. | Pocket's two recordings, or any clip in your bin                          | About 1.1 GB |

The Model picker shows each one with a subtitle: "Built-in speakers · fast", "Any voice, from a
recording", "Studio voice · slow". With Chatterbox picked, an **Expression** row under the script
inserts the three tags at the cursor.

The **Voice** list leads each name with a round picture or initial. For Pocket TTS and Chatterbox
it lists your [saved voices](/guides/sounds-and-voices#voices) after the named ones and ends in
**Clone from a sample…**, which turns on the sample voice below.

## Clone a voice

Pocket TTS and Chatterbox can read in any voice they are given a few seconds of.

1. Import a recording of the voice, or use a clip that is already in the bin. Ten seconds of
   clean speech is plenty.
2. In Text to speech, pick **Pocket TTS** or **Chatterbox Turbo**.
3. Turn on **Use sample voice** (or pick **Clone from a sample…** at the end of the Voice list).
4. Pick the recording under **Sample**, and drag **Voice from** to a point where the person is
   talking: the model listens to the ten seconds from there.
5. Generate.

If a clip with sound is selected on the timeline when you open the panel, it is already the
sample. The panel refuses with "Pick a sample with a voice in it" when the clip is silent.

To keep the voice for other projects, type a name under **Save this voice** and press **Save**: it
joins the Voice list of every project and the Library's Audio page. See
[your sounds and voices](/guides/sounds-and-voices#voices). A **Style** picked on the Audio page
(Chipmunk, Robot and the rest) shows under the voice; the read lands with its effects on the
clip.

> [!TIP]
> Chatterbox has no voice of its own. With Pocket TTS installed it borrows Bria; otherwise
> turn on Use sample voice and give it a recording.

## Put the words on screen too

Select the generated clip and click **Generate Captions**: the read is transcribed back into
titles, timed to the voice. See [auto-captions](/guides/auto-captions).
