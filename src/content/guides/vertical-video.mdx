Make the project 9:16 (the New project sheet's **Shape**, or the Inspector's **Modify** button
for an existing timeline) and a 16:9 clip sits in the middle of the frame with black above and
below it. In 0.2.7 the way to fill those bars is by hand: put a second copy of the clip on a lane
below, scale it up and give it a blur effect. The canvas below does it in one step.

## The canvas

> [!NOTE]
> **New in 0.2.8 (coming in the next release).** Everything on this page from here on is on the
> app's main branch and not in 0.2.7.

Select a video or still on the **main lane**, the lowest lane that holds a picture, and open the
Inspector's **Video** tab, **Basic** page. The **Canvas** section sets what fills the frame where
the picture does not reach:

| Background | What it draws                                                                               |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **None**   | Black                                                                                       |
| **Blur**   | The clip's own picture, enlarged to cover the frame and blurred. **Softness** sets how much |
| **Color**  | One flat colour                                                                             |
| **Image**  | A still from the bin, enlarged to cover the frame. Import a picture first to pick one       |

**Apply to all** gives every picture on the main lane this clip's background, as one undo step.

The background is drawn behind the main lane only, where CapCut has its main track. Titles,
shapes and pictures on the lanes above sit on top of a frame that is already filled. It travels
with its clip through fades, dissolves and wipes, and a picture that already covers the frame
gets none. The monitor and the export draw it the same way.

On a phone, **Canvas** on the bottom strip (and on a picture's strip) opens a sheet of tiles:
four softness steps and twelve colour swatches, with Fit and Fill at its head for a
clip, and Apply to all.

## Fit and Fill

The **Transform** section has a **Size** row with two buttons, also on a clip's right-click menu
as **Fit to frame** and **Fill frame**:

- **Fit**: the whole picture inside the frame, as large as it goes. Every picture starts this way.
- **Fill**: the frame covered, the picture centred and cut at the edges that overhang.

Neither stretches the picture. If the clip's scale or position has keyframes, Fit and Fill add a
key at the playhead rather than replacing the animation.

Scripts reach the same with the `frameClip`, `setTimelineBackground` and `updateClip` edits; see
[Edit commands](/docs/api/edits#framing-and-background).
