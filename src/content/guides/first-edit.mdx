## 1. Make a project

Concat opens on the start screen. Click **New project** on the left, or the first card.

<Figure
  src={start}
  alt="The Concat start screen: a New project button on the left rail, a Projects grid with a New project card and the recent projects"
>
  The start screen. Everything you have opened on this machine is listed under Projects.
</Figure>

The New project sheet asks for a **name**, a **location** (a `Concat` folder on your Desktop by
default), the **shape** of the frame (16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:3), the **size** (720p to 4K, or
Custom) and the **frame rate**. Sizes are the short edge, so 9:16 at 1080p is 1080 by 1920, a
vertical video. Click **Create**. The project is a folder, and the edit autosaves into it from
now on.

> [!TIP]
> Frame and rate can be changed later per timeline from the Inspector's **Modify** button, so
> do not agonise. Pick 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube.

## 2. Bring footage in

The editor opens with the **Library** on the left, the **Preview** in the middle, the
**Inspector** on the right and the **Timeline** underneath. (From 0.2.8 the Inspector runs the
full height of the right side; see [where everything is](/guides/the-window#the-layout).)

<Figure
  src={editor}
  alt="The Concat editor with the Library, the Preview, the Inspector and the Timeline"
>
  The editor. Nothing is selected, so the Inspector shows the project's details.
</Figure>

Click **Import** at the top of the Library (or press <kbd class="kbd">⌘I</kbd> on a Mac,

<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+I</kbd> elsewhere), or drag files from your desktop onto the Library. Video,
audio and pictures all land in the bin under **All media**.

To get a file onto the timeline, **double-click** its card: it lands at the playhead. Or drag the
card onto a lane to put it exactly where you want. A single click only previews the file in the
monitor.

## 3. Cut

The tray above the lanes holds the editing tools.

<Figure
  src={tray}
  alt="The timeline tray: add track, undo, redo, split at playhead, split tool, merge, delete, freeze menu, pan tool, trim follow, snap, magnetic timeline, generate captions, text to speech"
  width="48rem"
>
  The tray, left to right: add a track, undo and redo, split at playhead, the razor, merge, delete,
  the freeze and rotate menu, the pan tool, trim follow, snap, the magnetic switch, Generate
  Captions and Text to Speech.
</Figure>

- Scrub by dragging on the ruler, or press <kbd class="kbd">Space</kbd> to play.
- Put the playhead where you want the cut and press <kbd class="kbd">⌘B</kbd>
  (<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+B</kbd>): every clip under the playhead splits. From 0.2.8 it splits
  the selected clips, or everything under the playhead when nothing is selected, and
  <kbd class="kbd">Q</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">W</kbd> delete the part of a clip left or right of
  the playhead.
- Or press <kbd class="kbd">B</kbd> for the razor and click a clip where you want it cut.
- Drag a clip's edge to trim it. Drag the clip itself to move it.
- Select a clip and press <kbd class="kbd">Delete</kbd>. With the **Magnetic timeline** switch
  on, what follows slides into the gap; off, the gap stays. <kbd class="kbd">Shift+Delete</kbd>
  always closes the gap.

Every move is one undo step: <kbd class="kbd">⌘Z</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">⇧⌘Z</kbd>.

## 4. Add a title

Open the Library's **Text** page and click **Default title**, or one of the looks under
**Styles**. A title lands at the playhead on the first free lane above the picture. Type into the
**Content** box in the Inspector; drag the title around on the monitor to place it. The
[titles guide](/guides/titles-and-text) covers fonts, shadows and background plates.

Want the words spoken rather than written? [Text to speech](/guides/text-to-speech) is one
button away on the tray. Want the words that are already spoken on screen?
[Auto-captions](/guides/auto-captions).

## 5. Export

Click the blue **Export** button at the top right of the window (or press

<kbd class="kbd">⌘E</kbd>). The sheet starts at the timeline's own size and rate with **Balanced**
quality, which is right for almost everything (from 0.2.8, a recommended **Bitrate** in its place).
Pick a name, click **Export**, and the file goes to your Movies folder. The [export
guide](/guides/export) has the rest: codecs, 10-bit, bitrate.

That is the whole loop. Everything else on these pages is a detail of one of these five steps.
