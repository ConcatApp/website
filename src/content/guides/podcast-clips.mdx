A one-hour conversation holds a dozen thirty-second moments that would travel on their own. Here
is a way to cut them in Concat without starting a new project for each: one project holds the
full episode and every clip, each on its own timeline.

## Set up one project for the episode

Make a 16:9 project at your recording's size and frame rate, and import the episode. If the video
and the sound were recorded separately, put both on the timeline and line them up by a clap or a
clear word at the start. For
a quick check of what you recorded (size, frame rate, codec, length) before you begin, the
[video file inspector](/tools/video-info) reads the file in your browser.

## Find the moments

Play the episode through, or transcribe it first: [auto-captions](/guides/auto-captions) on the
whole recording puts every phrase on the timeline as a title, so you can skim the words and jump
straight to the good part. Delete the caption lane afterwards, or keep it for the clip.

At each moment you want, place a marker by splitting the clip there
(<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+B</kbd>, <kbd class="kbd">⌘B</kbd> on a Mac). You will copy these stretches
into the vertical timelines next.

## A vertical timeline per clip

Click the plus beside the timeline tabs to add a timeline, double-click its tab to name it after
the moment, and set it to 9:16 at 1080p with the Inspector's **Modify** button. Copy the stretch
from the episode timeline (<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+C</kbd>) and paste it into the new one
(<kbd class="kbd">Ctrl+V</kbd>); clips paste across timelines at the playhead. Each
timeline keeps its own frame and rate, and the Export sheet exports whichever one is open; see
[several timelines](/guides/cut-on-the-timeline#several-timelines).

## Make the speaker fill the frame

A wide shot of two people looks lost in a tall frame. Select the clip and press **Fill** in the
Inspector's Transform section: the picture is enlarged to cover the frame, cut at the sides.
Then nudge **Position X** until the person talking is centred. When the other person answers,
split the clip there and move the second half's position to them. Keyframes on Position X move
the frame smoothly between the two instead; see [keyframes](/guides/keyframes).

If you would rather keep the whole wide shot, set **Canvas** to **Blur** to fill the bars with
the picture itself; see [vertical video](/guides/vertical-video).

## Clean up the voice

Podcast audio recorded in a room benefits from all three switches in the Inspector's **Audio**
tab: **Normalize loudness** brings every clip to the same broadcast level, **Reduce noise** takes
out hiss and hum, and **Enhance voice** adds presence. They run as the clip plays, with nothing to
download. See [clean up a voice](/guides/clean-up-a-voice).

## Caption it

Select the clip and press the **T** on the timeline tray. Captions land one phrase at a time,
timed to the words. Make them large and place them in the middle of the frame, where the apps
draw nothing over them. Style one, then style them all at once; see
[titles and text](/guides/titles-and-text).

## Export each clip

Open a clip's timeline and click **Export**: 1080 × 1920, H.264, the bitrate it starts at. Then
open the next tab and export again. Every file is free of watermarks.

If you only need the sound of an episode as an MP3 for a podcast feed,
[extract the audio](/tools/extract-audio) from the video in your browser.
