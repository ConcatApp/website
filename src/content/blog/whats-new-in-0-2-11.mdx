Concat 0.2.11 is out. Cuts land exactly on the frame, the preview keeps drawing when a
computer's graphics chip gives up, and Concat now keeps a log you can read. Here is what you get
when you update.

## Clean cuts

Split or trim a clip anywhere, even between two frames, and the cut is clean: the clips on both
sides meet on the same frame, in the preview and in the exported file. Footage that is slower
than your export, or slowed down, holds its last frame to the end.

**Merge** now takes clips cut between frames too, both halves of a split clip included.

## A preview that keeps drawing

When a computer's graphics chip stops answering, Concat lets it go, tries it again, then steps
down to the next way of drawing. The last step is the processor itself, which always draws. On
that step the monitor shows your picture without effects and says so once, and your edit goes on.

It is quick, too. On a Mac with Apple's M5 chip, a 1280x720 clip under six captions draws at the
monitor's size in 3.6 ms on ten cores.

## A log you can read

Concat writes down what it does, and now you can read it. Every line says when, how serious,
what about and what happened:

- what you did, in the words the interface uses
- how each run started and how it ended
- each export: what was asked, every stage, and one closing line that says where the time went

Each line is in the file as soon as it is written, so the log is whole whatever happens next. It
never holds your text or captions.

On a computer, **Settings › About › Show the log** opens it. On an Android phone,
**Settings › About › Share the log** sends this run's log and the one before it in two taps.

## Faster

Titles, shapes and photos are now read once for an export and kept ready, not read again for
every frame. We measured it before and after on the same Mac with Apple's M5 chip: a 60 second
1080p export with a cut every 6.7 seconds and a title every 6 seconds.

| What Concat does                         | Before | After  | Faster   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | -------- |
| Draw every frame of the 60 second export | 5.5 s  | 4.1 s  | **1.3x** |
| Read and draw one frame                  | 4.1 ms | 3.6 ms | **1.1x** |

An emoji's picture is decoded once and kept, so a title full of emoji paints as it should. A
speed curve is prepared once, so playing and exporting a clip with one costs almost nothing extra.

## Smaller things that got better

- On Windows and Linux, the window can be minimised, maximised and moved while Export, Settings
  or another full-window page is up. Close is dimmed during an export, so one click in the corner
  cannot cancel it.
- **Show in folder** and **Remove** on a recent project's card stay under the pointer.
- Duplicate is one undo step, for one clip or many.
- A cut on a step of a speed curve keeps the jump.
- Emoji drawn in layers and gradients show in colour in titles.
- Captions on an Intel Mac are generated on the processor.
- On a phone, the row of tabs in Settings sits evenly.
- 25 languages are level with English: 3,288 strings translated.

## For scripts and agents

- A finished export reports every frame as rendered.
- A speed curve sent through the API is stored in order and in range.

The [developer docs](/docs) cover every method and edit command.
