We spent the last two days finding where Concat makes you wait, then fixing the biggest
waits first. Every number on this page was measured before and after each change, on the same
machine (a Mac with Apple's M5 chip), with the same files. The changes are in Concat's code now
and arrive in the next release.

## The numbers

| What you do                                          | Before                         | After          | Faster   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------- | -------- |
| A title or caption appears for the first time        | 77 ms                          | 1.7 ms         | **45x**  |
| Open a project with 500 captions                     | about 38 seconds               | under 1 second | **42x**  |
| Drag a title on a timeline with 500 captions         | 754 ms a step                  | 0.8 ms a step  | **940x** |
| Show an effect for the first time after installing   | 86 ms on average, up to 583 ms | under 1 ms     | **150x** |
| Import a 4K clip and a 1080p clip                    | 5.0 seconds                    | 3.3 seconds    | **1.5x** |
| Export 10 seconds with 10 cuts on two stacked tracks | 2.8 seconds                    | 2.3 seconds    | **1.2x** |

A millisecond (ms) is a thousandth of a second. Anything over about 16 ms is a frame you can
see the editor miss.

## Captions and titles

Auto-captions turns a long recording into hundreds of captions, and each one is a title Concat
has to paint. A title used to be painted on a canvas the size of the whole frame, and its soft
shadow was blurred across all of it, even though the words cover a thin strip. Now the shadow
is blurred only where it falls, the finished picture is saved faster, and Concat paints many
titles at once, using the whole computer.

A transcribed interview with 500 captions now opens in under a second instead of over half a
minute, and dragging a title stays smooth however many captions sit on the timeline. Concat
also keeps more of the painted titles in memory on computers that have more to give, so moving
one title no longer repaints the others.

The titles come out exactly as before. Tests compare every pixel of the new painting with the
old one.

## Effects

Each effect has to be prepared before its first frame can be drawn, and that used to happen the
first time you used it: the first look you hovered in the Library, the first frame of an effect
you had just added. Now Concat prepares every effect in the background while the window opens,
so a look shows the moment you point at it.

## Importing

When you import a clip, Concat draws the strip of small pictures you see along it on the
timeline. For pictures that small, it now skips work you could never see at that size. An
import of a 4K clip and a 1080p clip finished in 3.3 seconds instead of 5.0, doing less than
half the work, which leaves more of your computer for playback while a big import
fills in.

## Exporting

An export used to read each clip's footage one piece at a time, and every cut paused while the
next file opened. Now each clip is read alongside the drawing, and a clip's file is opened half
a second before its cut. Exports with many cuts or with clips stacked over each other finished
up to **1.2x** faster, frame for frame identical to before.

Every number here was measured before and after the change, and titles and exports come out exactly as before. Follow along on [GitHub](https://github.com/jub0t/Concat) or in the
[Discord](https://discord.gg/DVuPfpXfqP), and [download Concat](/#download) to try it.
